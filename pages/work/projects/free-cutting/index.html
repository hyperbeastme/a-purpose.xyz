Process

For this look, I used grey-washed and black silk to create a free-cut dress using Julian Roberts’ subtraction-cutting method. I envisioned the wearer existing within a post-apocalyptic desert environment, where the garment provides coverage while remaining lightweight and breathable. The dress is paired with a scarf and utility bag made from the same fabric, extending the material language throughout the look.

This project also marked a shift in how I understood my own visual language. I had consistently gravitated toward darker colors and neutral tones, but had not previously considered that preference an intentional part of my design identity. Through this look, I began to embrace that restraint rather than feeling the need to justify or move away from it.

Take two different woven fabrics, approximately 3.5 yards each, and sew them together selvedge to selvedge. Position the front and back pattern pieces opposite one another, shoulder to shoulder. Draw a continuous line connecting one side seam to the other, then cut along the traced line. Join the resulting side seams to form the garment.
